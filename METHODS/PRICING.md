# Pricing — Value-Based, Client-Stated, Milestone-Paid

Status: canon method. Adopted 2026-09-21 by Director.
Applies to: done-for-you funnel service, Porter onboarding and custom work, any AI or automation build sold to a client. For rank-and-rent rent-setting, see "Applying to rank-and-rent" below.
Source: distilled from Nate Herk, "Codex beginner to pro" course (2026), final two chapters, which credit pricing consultant Jonathan Stark for several principles. Figures below labeled "source example" are the source's own worked numbers, not Kinesthetic Marketing data.

## Principles
1. Cost does not justify price. Value justifies price. Your costs set the floor you walk away below; the client's value sets the ceiling; price lands between.
2. Never bill hourly past the first two or three engagements. Hourly pays more for slower work and less as tooling makes you faster.
3. The client states the cost of the problem in their own words before any number is said. Price is then a fraction of a number they already said.
4. Choose one KPI with the client before accepting payment: a baseline and a target, objective and trackable. A subjective goal ("feel less busy") cannot be proven delivered.
5. Diagnose the constraint before scoping the build. Ask "If you had 10x the business tomorrow, what breaks first?" (supply-constrained) or "What would you do to 10x the business coming in?" (demand-constrained). Then stay silent and let them answer. What they ask for is often not what removes the constraint.

## Discovery questions
- Why this: does the requested build actually produce the outcome they want?
- Why now: what urgency or closing window makes this worth money now?
- Why me: why not build it internally? Hear the objection on the call, not after the proposal.
- Sizing when they won't share numbers: How long does this take today? How many people touch it? What happens when it goes wrong? How many per week/month? How many locations?

## The formula
Price = 10-20% of projected first-year value. The client should be able to see how they get roughly 10x their investment in year one.
Value sources, in order of strength:
- Hard dollars lost (errors, missed calls, no-shows, churn)
- Revenue enabled (conversions x value per conversion)
- Labor hours returned (hours x loaded hourly cost)
Hours are the weakest basis; prefer bottom-line impact once experience supports the estimate.

Source example (appointment-setting agent): 20 leads/week x 1 hour each x $40/hr loaded = $800/week = $41,600/year. Build priced at $5,500 (about 13%). Maintenance $400/month.

## Proposal structure
1. Three short paragraphs, none about you: where the business is now in their words and numbers; where they said they want to be; why your system gets them there.
2. Three tiers at rising percentages of first-year value, each with materially different scope or outcome. The middle tier is the intended choice. Three options change the decision from "whether" to "which."
3. Expected monthly run cost (API, hosting) with the volume assumption stated beside it. Estimate, not guarantee.

## Payment and milestones
- Split into stages about 30 days apart: start, midpoint milestone, production milestone. You never carry much more than 30 days of unpaid work.
- Every milestone is objective. Good: "Owner sends a question; the agent pulls from the database and responds within one minute." Bad: "Agent working as expected."
- Scope creep is a buying signal. Log it to a v2 backlog; do not absorb it into current milestones.

## Budget pushback
Cut scope, never price. "That budget buys this smaller v1; the rest is scoped for the next phase once v1 is earning." Discounting on a frown teaches the client that your price moves.

## Maintenance
A flat monthly figure that guarantees the build keeps doing what the scope says: fixes for breakage, API changes, model changes, edge cases within scope. New functionality is a separate engagement. Confirm the flat figure exceeds your expected maintenance labor for that build.

## Run costs and testing
- Client's accounts, client's card, for production API and subscription costs. Your fee covers design, build, and testing.
- Pre-production testing runs on your accounts; fold expected testing spend into the build price. Swap to client keys at production.

## After production: measure
Capture the KPI baseline before the build and report the after-state at 1, 2, and 3 months. The source reported losing the follow-on engagement on a project where this was skipped. The measured delta is what justifies the retainer.

## Compliance
Never guarantee results or dollar outcomes. Projections are labeled estimates with their assumptions shown. (Consistent with the Director's standing rule against guaranteed dollar amounts.)

## Applying to rank-and-rent
Rent is value-based on the same logic: value to the contractor = qualified leads/month x their close rate x their average job value. The contractor states close rate and job value; rent is a fraction of the resulting monthly value. Measured lead counts from the property are the KPI.
